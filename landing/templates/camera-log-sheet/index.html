What a camera log sheet is

A camera log, or camera report, is the record the camera department keeps of what each card holds. It ties every clip file name to a scene, setup, and take, and notes the technical detail, timecode, frame rate, resolution, lens, so the DIT can offload cleanly and the editor can match footage to the script without opening every clip to check.

The one rule that matters most: the clip name on the sheet has to match the clip name on the card exactly. Get that right and the whole downstream chain, offload, sync, edit, lines up on its own.

What is on the sheet

  • Roll / cardThe card or roll ID this clip lives on.
  • Clip / fileThe clip file name, exactly as the camera wrote it.
  • Scene / setupThe scene and camera setup the clip belongs to.
  • TakeThe take number, so it matches the continuity sheet.
  • TC in / outClip start and end timecode.
  • FPSFrame rate, so nothing gets conformed wrong.
  • ResolutionCapture resolution and format.
  • Lens / mmLens and focal length for the setup.
  • ND / stopFiltration or stop, if your workflow tracks it.
  • Good takeA box to check the keepers at a glance.

How a pro fills it in

  1. Head the sheet with the card

    Production, camera body or letter, operator, DIT or loader, and the roll or card ID. One sheet per card so nothing crosses over.

  2. Log the clip name exactly

    Write the file name the way the camera wrote it. No shorthand, no drift, no off-by-one.

  3. Tie it to scene and take

    Scene, setup, take, so the editor can match footage to the continuity and script.

  4. Record timecode and format

    Timecode in and out, frame rate, resolution, lens, and ND or stop if you track it.

  5. Check the good takes

    Tick the good-take box on the keepers and note anything the editor or colorist should know.

When to use it

Use it any time you are shooting to cards and someone has to account for them: narrative, documentary, commercial, or multi-camera. Start a fresh sheet for every card, keep it with the camera or the DIT cart, and hand it off at wrap with the media. When a card comes up short or a clip goes missing, this sheet is the paper trail that finds it.

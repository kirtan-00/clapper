Flagging the keeper

How does the editor know which take the director actually wanted?

The flag has to survive the call, not just the moment. On set, the standard is the circle take: the script supervisor circles a take number on the script when the director calls it out loud. Off set, that flag is only useful if it rides all the way to the edit without a phone call, an email, or someone's memory of who said what on day four. A shot log that carries the flag on every export, PDF, XML markers, and CSV, means the editor sees it the moment they open the project.

The failure mode this replaces

An editor scrubbing eight takes of the same line reading, all of them competent, none of them obviously the one, with no record of what happened on the day. The director said something after take five, everyone on set heard it, and none of it is in the footage or on the page in front of the editor now. So the editor calls the AD, or the script supervisor, and waits for someone to check their memory of a specific afternoon three weeks back. It is not a rare scenario. It is what happens by default any time the flag depends on a person remembering it rather than a record carrying it.

What actually needs to happen at the moment of the call

The flag needs to be marked the instant the director says it, not reconstructed later from memory. That is the whole mechanism behind the circled take as a convention in the first place: mark it now, while it is unambiguous, rather than trying to remember it at dailies or at wrap. What that flag is called day to day, GOLD, circled, starred, matters less than whether it is captured the moment it happens. The history of the circle take itself, and how the mark works on paper, is covered on the circle takes explained page.

The one thing that breaks this: deciding to flag it "later, from memory." By the time later arrives, the take that got circled and the take everyone half-remembers being good are not always the same take.

Where the flag needs to show up for the editor

Not in one place. In three, so it survives whichever file the editor opens first: as a status on the take row in the PDF shot log, with the good takes summarized up front rather than buried in a full day's list; as text on the Premiere marker itself, so it is visible the second the timeline opens, no cross-referencing a separate document; and as a column in the CSV, for anyone working from the data rather than a timeline or a PDF. Three formats, one flag, set once at the moment the director called it.

Marked once, everywhere

Flag it the moment it happens

Star the keeper take on set and the flag carries through to every export automatically: the PDF summary, the Premiere marker text, and the CSV status column.

Is there a free alternative to Shot Notes X?

Shot Notes X reads a CSV or an XML from ScriptE or Movie Slate and turns those notes into clip markers in Final Cut Pro or Premiere. It is a paid, Mac-only desktop app, and it only reads notes that already exist in one of those formats. Clapper logs the take itself for free, from a phone on Android or iPhone, no account needed. At wrap, a free account exports a Premiere XML with the markers already placed, no Mac required, drawing on a small free quota rather than an unlimited one. No separate note-taking app to buy first.

What Shot Notes X actually does

It is a real, useful, narrow tool. You take notes on location in ScriptE, Movie Slate, or a spreadsheet, export that as a CSV or XML, and Shot Notes X matches those notes against the clips already imported into Final Cut Pro X or Premiere, then writes them in as markers. It is a matching utility sitting between a notes file you already have and a timeline you already built. It does not take the notes itself, and it does not touch a camera card directly.

That is worth saying plainly, because it means the comparison here is not app for app. It is: keep the two-step version, note-taking app plus a separate matching pass, or collapse both steps into one phone-native log that already knows the clip it belongs to the moment you tap it.

Where the free version is different

Clapper skips the middle step. You do not keep a separate note file and match it to clips afterward by timecode. The note is created at the exact moment of the take, on the phone that is usually already at the camera cart or in the script supervisor's hand, and it is scene, shot, and take scoped by construction, not reconstructed later from a spreadsheet column.

That also removes the failure mode a matching pass is prone to: a note whose timecode is slightly off, or a take number that got fat-fingered on the page, quietly binding to the wrong clip. A tap made during the take is stamped against that take's own clock, so there is nothing to match after the fact.

What you get at wrap

A Premiere XML with a marker on every clip at the exact frame something was tagged, named from the shot code, the tag, and the label. Alongside it, a PDF shot log built for the director and production office, and a CSV with one row per tagged moment for anyone who wants the data rather than a timeline. All three come out of the same taps, no re-entry between them. Logging the take is free with no account; exporting needs a free sign-in and draws on a small free quota (2 Premiere XML, 5 PDF, 5 CSV). The mechanics of the export itself, including why a good take shows up twice on the timeline without duplicating your media bin, are covered in full on the shot log to Premiere XML page.

When Shot Notes X is still the right tool

If your notes already live in ScriptE or Movie Slate output and your whole pipeline is Mac and Final Cut, Shot Notes X remains a fine glue layer between what you already produce and your timeline. It solves a real matching problem for a workflow that already has structured notes. Clapper's angle is for the far more common case on smaller productions: nobody is running a dedicated notes app yet, and the phone in someone's pocket is the only device guaranteed to be at the camera the whole day.

4. Advertising measurement: the Meta Pixel

Clapper is free and has no budget for a marketing department, so we run ads on Instagram. To know whether an ad brought a working crew member or just burned money, this site and the app load the Meta Pixel, a small piece of tracking code from Meta Platforms (Facebook and Instagram). This is genuine third-party ad tracking and we would rather say so plainly than bury it.

What the Pixel sets

  • _fbp — a first-party cookie holding a random browser ID. It is what lets Meta recognise the same browser across visits and match it back to an ad. It is set on every visit, ad or not, and typically expires about three months after your last visit.
  • _fbc — set only if you arrived by clicking one of our ads, because the ad link carries a click identifier (fbclid) in the URL. It records which click brought you. Same rough lifetime.

What is sent to Meta

  • A page view each time you open a page on this site or in the app: the URL, the referring page, your browser and device type, and your IP address (Meta receives this by virtue of the request).
  • A handful of product milestones, so we can tell a curious visitor from someone who actually used the thing — an event when a project is created, an event when a file is exported, and a few similar counts.

What is never sent to Meta

  • Nothing from your script or shot list. Not a scene, not a line of dialogue, not a slugline.
  • No project names, no file names, no clip numbers, no take data, no tags, no notes.
  • No email address, no name, and no Google account identifier. We do not use Meta's Advanced Matching, so we do not hand Meta hashed contact details either.
  • Nothing at all from the PDF, XML, or CSV you export — those files are built on your device and never pass through Meta.

The milestone events are counts, not contents. "A project was created" is the whole message; which project, and what is in it, we could not tell Meta even if we wanted to, because it never leaves your device.

Meta uses this for its own purposes too. Meta is not just running a counter for us. It may combine what the Pixel reports with the profile it already holds about you, use it to target ads to you and to people it thinks are like you, and use it to improve its own advertising systems. That side of it is governed by Meta's own rules, not ours — see the Meta Privacy Policy and the Meta Cookies Policy.

How to switch it off

All of these work, and none of them break Clapper. Logging takes, Script Mode, and every export keep working with the Pixel fully blocked — it does nothing except report to Meta.

  • Use a browser that already blocks it. Safari (Intelligent Tracking Prevention) and Firefox (Enhanced Tracking Protection, on by default) restrict or bin these cookies without you doing anything. Brave and DuckDuckGo's browser block the Pixel outright. On Chrome, the Pixel runs unless you stop it.
  • Install a content blocker. uBlock Origin, AdGuard, or Privacy Badger block the Pixel script before it loads. On iOS, any Safari content-blocker app does the same.
  • Block or clear the cookies. In your browser's settings, block third-party and cross-site tracking cookies, or clear site data for this domain. Clearing site data will also clear your Clapper projects, so export first.
  • Turn off ad tracking inside Meta. In the Facebook or Instagram app, open Settings → Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Your activity off Meta technologies. From there you can see what sites have reported activity about you, disconnect it from your account, and switch off future activity. Ad targeting itself is at facebook.com/adpreferences.

Be clear about what each one buys you. Blocking the script or the cookie stops the data reaching Meta from your browser at all. The Meta-side controls are different: they mostly stop the data being used to target ads at you, and Meta may still receive and count it in aggregate. If you want none of it to leave your browser, use a blocker.

The Pixel is on the marketing site, the app, and our free template pages. If we ever add another advertising or analytics tracker, it will be named on this page before it ships.

5. Who processes data on our behalf (sub-processors)

  • Google (Sign-in with Google) — authenticates you and provides your email address. Governed by Google's own privacy policy.
  • Supabase — our backend host, which stores your account email, your export counters, and anonymous usage events.
  • Cloudflare (Turnstile) — a privacy-friendly bot check that runs when a gated action starts, to stop automated abuse. It is not an advertising cookie.
  • Groq — an AI inference provider used only in Script Mode to extract scenes from the screenplay text you import. It processes that text to return the result and does not receive your account details.

We share the minimum necessary with each and do not sell personal data to anyone. Meta is deliberately not on this list. Meta does not merely process pixel data on our behalf — it also uses it for its own advertising purposes, which makes it an independent recipient rather than a sub-processor. See Advertising measurement.

6. How long we keep it

  • Account email & export counters — kept while your account exists; deleted when you ask us to delete your account.
  • Anonymous usage events — automatically deleted after 90 days.
  • Rate-limiting records — automatically deleted within minutes.
  • Meta Pixel cookies — stored in your browser, not by us, and typically expiring about three months after your last visit. You can delete them sooner at any time. How long Meta keeps what it has already received is set by Meta's own retention policy, not ours.
  • On-device data — controlled entirely by you, on your device.

8. Security

Data in transit is encrypted over HTTPS. Backend tables use row-level security so a signed-in user can only read their own row, and the authoritative counters are written only by trusted server code. No system is perfectly secure, but we keep the data we hold deliberately small.

Cookies & Storage Notice

Most of what Clapper stores in your browser exists only to make the app work. One item — the Meta Pixel cookie — is advertising, and is listed as such.

Strictly necessary

  • Local storage / IndexedDB — stores your projects and takes on your device so the app works offline. Never sent to us.
  • Sign-in session — if you sign in with Google, a session token is stored locally so you stay signed in. Cleared when you sign out.
  • Cloudflare Turnstile — may set a short-lived token when a gated action runs, purely to confirm you are not a bot. Not an advertising cookie.

Advertising

  • _fbp (Meta Pixel) — a random browser ID that lets Meta recognise this browser and match it to one of our ads. Set on every visit; typically expires about three months after your last one.
  • _fbc (Meta Pixel) — set only when you arrive by clicking one of our ads, recording which click brought you.

These two are neither strictly necessary nor user-initiated: the app works perfectly with them blocked. Section 4 of the Privacy Policy sets out exactly what they send, what they never send, and several ways to switch them off.

Clapper does not currently show a cookie-consent banner. You can clear or block any of the above at any time from your browser settings, or with a content blocker. Note that clearing site data for this domain will also wipe your Clapper projects, so export first.

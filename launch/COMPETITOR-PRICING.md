## 2. Where Clapper's draft pricing sits

Draft: INR 699 for one project (one-time), INR 1,999 for five projects (one-time), INR 999/month for 2 projects plus 10 hours of podcast recording.

Rough conversion for scale only (not a sourced FX rate, just for orientation): INR 699 ≈ $8, INR 1,999 ≈ $23, INR 999 ≈ $11.50.

**The closest functional match in the entire field is Script Evolution.** It is a per-shooting-day, one-time-credit iPad app for script supervisors, with no subscription, and past projects stay free to open and export forever. Its single-day credit is $7.99 - almost exactly what INR 699 converts to. Its 5-day bundle is $32.99 (≈INR 2,870), which makes Clapper's INR 1,999 for five projects noticeably cheaper than the nearest priced analog for the same core job, despite Clapper adding Premiere XML, Resolve FCPXML, CSV and PDF export that Script Evolution does not offer.

Against the podcast side, INR 999/month for 10 hours of recording sits below Auphonic's cheapest recurring tier (9 hrs for $13 ≈ INR 1,130) and well below Descript's cheapest paid tier ($16-24/mo ≈ INR 1,400-2,100) or Riverside's Standard plan ($19/mo ≈ INR 1,650 for 5 hrs). Clapper is proposing more hours for less money than any recording/processing tool in the category, though it is worth noting Clapper's podcast mode logs chapters and moments, it does not record, transcribe, or edit audio - so it is not doing the same job as Descript or Riverside, only a lighter slice of it.

Against Indian SaaS benchmarks generally, prosumer/indie tiers cluster at INR 299-499/month, and a "growth" tier for small teams sits at INR 999-1,999/month. Clapper's monthly price lands exactly inside that growth band. The one-project price of INR 699 is below even the "starter" indie band, which fits a single-use, single-shoot buyer.

## 3. Verdict: underpriced, overpriced, or right

**India: about right, arguably slightly cautious.** INR 699 for one project and INR 999/month sit inside or below the normal Indian indie-SaaS range (INR 299-1,999/month), and both are a rounding error against the paper alternative they replace. A single US script supervisor day rate ($325-450, source below) already costs more than an entire year of Clapper's monthly plan (999 x 12 = INR 11,988, roughly $138). Indian assistant editor time alone runs INR 300-2,000/hour. There is no price resistance risk here; if anything the five-project bundle at INR 1,999 could likely go to INR 2,499-2,999 without losing the Indian solo-creator buyer, since it still undercuts Script Evolution's five-day bundle once converted.

**International: underpriced.** Every subscription competitor with a US/UK/EU customer base prices at $9.99/month minimum (MovieSlate Pro), rising to $19-35/month for anything with recording or editing (Riverside, Descript), and $42+/month for full production management (StudioBinder). Clapper's INR 999/month converts to roughly $11.50, which undercuts even the cheapest one-purpose competitor (MovieSlate Pro Features at $9.99/mo) despite bundling take-logging, podcast mode, and four export formats. Yamdu already proves geo-based pricing is normal and accepted in this exact category - it openly charges $185 in India versus $265 in the US for the same tier. Clapper should do the same rather than applying one INR-denominated price globally: charge international customers something closer to $15-25/month and $10-15/one-off project, not the direct INR-to-USD conversion of the Indian price.

## 4. The three competitors that matter most

1. **Script Evolution.** The only other product in this research that charges per shoot/per project rather than per seat or per month, and its price point already validates Clapper's INR 699 single-project number almost to the dollar. It is the sharpest reference point for whether Clapper's core pricing unit is sane.

2. **MovieSlate 8.** The dominant incumbent digital slate on iOS. Its base app is a $29.99 one-time purchase, which trains the entire slate-app buyer base to expect ownership, not a subscription, for the core logging tool. Clapper's mixed model (one-time project packs plus a monthly plan) has to coexist with that expectation on iOS specifically.

3. **Descript.** The dominant podcast/recording tool and the one most likely to be in a prospective podcast-mode user's head already. It sets the market's mental model for how podcast tools should be metered (hours/minutes, tiered by usage) and what a "normal" monthly price looks like ($16-35/mo). Clapper's podcast mode will be judged against this expectation even though it does a narrower job.

## 5. A pricing model worth stealing

Script Evolution's model is the one to copy, and Clapper has effectively already arrived at a version of it independently: sell shoot-days or projects as non-expiring one-time credits, and never re-charge for access to a project once it has been paid for - keep past logs readable, editable, and exportable forever even after credits run out. That "you own what you already logged" guarantee removes the single biggest objection a script supervisor or DIT has to a paid app: the fear of losing access to a live shoot's data mid-production over a billing lapse.

The secondary pattern worth borrowing is Yamdu's and Assemble's per-project add-on inside a subscription (buy one more project slot for a flat fee rather than jumping a whole pricing tier). That maps cleanly onto Clapper's "2 projects" monthly plan - letting a user buy a third project for a small top-up instead of forcing an upgrade could reduce churn at exactly the moment someone is mid-shoot and needs one more slot.

## 6. The paper alternative (the real anchor)

- **US script supervisor day rate:** roughly $325-450 for a 10-hour day using IATSE minimums as a floor, often negotiated higher. Freelance cable rates run about $40/hour, network rates about $60/hour. [Source](https://www.needacrew.com/blog/film-crew-day-rates-2026)
- **UK script supervisor:** average annual salary £53,117, equivalent to roughly £26/hour - a salary figure, not a day rate, but the closest published UK number. [Source](https://www.erieri.com/salary/job/script-supervisor/united-kingdom)
- **India script supervisor day rate:** not published as an isolated figure. The closest available data point is total 1-camera crew cost for a shoot day in India, cited at roughly $750-2,500, which includes the script supervisor as one line item among several. No standalone Indian script supervisor day rate was found - reporting the gap rather than estimating one.
- **India assistant editor / freelance video editor rate:** entry-level INR 300-600/hour, mid-level INR 600-1,200/hour, senior INR 1,200-2,000/hour. [Source](https://solohourly.com/rates/video-editor-rates-in-india)

Even using the most conservative of these numbers, one script supervisor day in any of these markets, or a couple of hours of assistant editor time, costs more than Clapper's entire one-project price. The software is not competing with other software on price - it is competing with a day rate, and it is priced two to three orders of magnitude below it.

## 7. Free tier shapes: who gates what

No competitor in this research gates **exports** specifically as the free-tier wall. The gates found cluster into three other shapes:

- **Gates usage/hours** (most common in podcast tools): Descript (1 hr/mo transcription), Riverside (2 hrs/mo + resolution cap + watermark), Auphonic (2 hrs/mo processed), Podcastle (1 hr record / 3 hrs transcribe). All podcast tools meter the free tier by time, not by project count or seats.
- **Gates project/document count**: Scriptation caps the free tier at 3 documents. Script Evolution gives exactly 1 free shoot-day credit, then requires payment per additional day, while explicitly keeping already-paid projects free to reopen forever.
- **Gates features, not volume**: Filmustage's free tier has unlimited use of non-AI tools but puts all AI processing behind the paid tier and a coin-credit system. StudioBinder's free plan is feature-limited rather than volume-limited, per available sources.

No product in this set gates by **seats** on its free tier - seat-based gating (Yamdu, Hindenburg, Scriptation's team tiers) only appears once a buyer is already paying.

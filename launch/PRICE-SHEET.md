# Clapper pricing, recommended

Synthesis of three research passes: PRICING-PSYCHOLOGY.md, COMPETITOR-PRICING.md,
USER-NEEDS.md. Every number below traces to one of them.

## The finding that reshapes everything

**The script supervisor role barely exists in India.** A Caravan investigation
found roughly two dedicated script supervisors in an industry producing about
1,000 films a year, and most Hindi film directors have never worked with one.

So the Indian buyer is NOT a continuity specialist on a feature. It is a 1st AD,
a DOP assistant, or the director, working the ad circuit: 50,000+ TVCs a year
across 3,000+ production houses. That person does MANY SHORT projects, not a few
long ones.

This inverts the cap maths. A cap of 2 projects a month is not generous to that
buyer, it is unusable. Their normal month is ten to twenty jobs.

FINAL, agreed 2026-08-27. The first draft put 15 projects in a single Rs 999
tier. That was wrong, and not because it cost anything: 15 projects is about
Rs 15 of Groq against Rs 999 of revenue. It was wrong because it removed the
upsell. One tier at 15 covers every individual user who exists, including the
ad-circuit 1st AD doing twenty jobs a month who is the most valuable customer
on the list, so individual ARPU would have been capped at Rs 999 with nothing
above it but a sales email. It also cannibalised the pack: Rs 999 for 15 is
Rs 67 a project against the pack's Rs 480, a 7x gap, so nobody doing more than
two projects would ever buy credits again.

Six at Rs 999 is Rs 166 a project, still 2.9x better than the pack, so a
regular working professional subscribes while the pack survives for the person
shooting once a quarter who will not commit monthly.

Twenty at Rs 2,499 is where the ad-circuit heavy hitter lands. That person
shoots 15 to 20 jobs a month and bills lakhs. The single-tier draft handed
them a 96% discount for no reason.

Studio Plus is the least evidenced number here. No source gives
productions-per-month for a 1st AD, so 20 is inferred from TVC volume rather
than measured. Ship it, watch where accounts actually pile up, move it.

Studio is ONE subscription carrying both meters. The pricing page splits by
persona so a film crew never reads the podcast meter and vice versa, but there
is only one plan behind both views. That kills the comprehension problem
without creating a fourth tier.

## Why each number

**Rs 699 per project.** Script Evolution charges USD 7.99 per shoot day, about
Rs 670. The only true per-project competitor lands within Rs 30 of this number
by coincidence, which is strong confirmation. Against a script supervisor day
rate or an assistant editor hour, it is a rounding error.

**Rs 2,399 for five, not Rs 1,999.** At Rs 1,999 the pack is 42.8% off the
single-credit rate. Practitioner consensus puts the credible bundle range at
10 to 20% off, with suspicion setting in past roughly 40%: "was the Rs 699 ever
real?" Rs 2,399 lands at 31%, which reads as a genuine deal rather than as
evidence the single price is padded. This corrects advice I gave earlier in the
session, where I argued the deeper discount was better. It is not.

**Rs 999 for 15 projects, not 2.** At 2 projects the subscription cost Rs 500 a
project while the pack cost Rs 400, so no filmmaker who did the arithmetic would
ever subscribe. At 15 it is unambiguously the heavy-user tier, which is the
entire stated goal. The ad-circuit buyer above crosses into it in week one.

**Free at 2 projects per month.** See the disagreement below.

**Podcast free at 3 hours, paid at 20.** Podcast usage is a cliff, not a bell
curve: about 90% of shows stop publishing after episode three and only 8% ever
reach ten episodes. The bottom 90% structurally cannot generate volume wherever
the cap sits, so a generous free tier costs almost nothing and buys goodwill
from the many who will never pay anyway. The 8% who survive are the customers.

**Every cap is a stated number with a live meter.** The no-unlimited instinct
holds up better than expected: fairness research says unpredictability damages
trust, not the existence of a limit. A visible "3 of 5 used" scores as well as
unlimited on predictability while still making heavy users pay. Never write
"fair use".

**Prices shown GST inclusive on self-serve**, itemised and exclusive only inside
the enterprise conversation. A price that grows at checkout reads as a trick to
an individual; a GST-registered company expects exclusive and reclaims it.

## Where the research disagrees with the spec

**The one-time free grant.** Two agents objected independently, which is the
strongest signal in this whole exercise.

The instruction was 2 projects once, forever, never refilling. The objection is
structural rather than from a study: this product's usage is bursty with
weeks-long gaps, so a permanent dead end lands the paywall at the moment a
returning user is BUSIEST and least receptive, mid-shoot, on a phone, with a
crew waiting. It also kills the free tier's second job, which is remaining a
live thing a happy user can hand to a colleague.

Recommendation: 2 projects per MONTH. If that feels too generous, the softer
version is a dormancy trickle, one credit back after four to six idle months,
which no heavy user can live on.

This is the owner's call. The rest of the sheet works either way.

## Not yet priced: international

Rs 999 converts to about USD 11.50, undercutting MovieSlate's cheapest add-on at
USD 9.99 despite doing more. The sheet above is correct for India and leaves
money on the table everywhere else. Yamdu already sets the precedent for
charging differently by country, USD 185 in India against USD 265 in the US for
the same tier. Worth a USD price list once Razorpay international or a merchant
of record is settled. Not urgent, but do not launch a US campaign on rupee
pricing.

## Numbers nobody could find

No source gives productions per month for a script supervisor or 1st AD. No
source quantifies takes per shoot day. Podcast recording hours are estimates
built from published length and an inferred raw-to-published ratio, not measured.
Several widely repeated pricing statistics traced back to unsourced marketing
blogs and are excluded here rather than dressed up as evidence.

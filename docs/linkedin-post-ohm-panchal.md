## Main post

🎬 **In 14 years of running productions, I can count on one hand the tools that actually changed how a set runs. Just added one to that list.**

Last week we shot a two-day ad campaign — 4 locations, 3 cameras, 80+ setups. Normally, the week after a shoot looks like this: the editor scrubbing through hours of footage, someone deciphering handwritten script-supervisor notes, and endless "which take was the good one?" calls.

This time? The editor opened Premiere, imported one file, and every good take was already on the timeline — with markers sitting right on the moments the director loved.

The tool is called **Clapper** → https://clapboard.duckdns.org/

It's deceptively simple. A phone-first app that lives in your pocket:

▶️ Tap ROLL when camera rolls, CUT when it cuts — or just say it out loud
⭐ One tap drops a timestamped tag — GOLD, FLUB, PICKUP — while the camera is still rolling
🎥 Clip numbers sync with what's actually on the camera card (Sony, RED, ARRI, Blackmagic, even iPhone)
📴 Runs fully offline. No account. No signal needed on a basement location or a highway shoot
📤 At wrap, it hands the editor three things: a Premiere timeline with markers, a CSV of every moment, and a printable PDF report

The part that got me: jam your cameras to time-of-day timecode, and the log lines up with the footage *automatically*. No syncing. No guesswork.

We cut our post-shoot logging time from hours to literally zero — because the log was being written while we were shooting.

Everyone talks about AI revolutionizing production. Sometimes the revolution is simpler: putting the script supervisor's notebook on a phone and making it talk directly to the edit bay.

My team isn't going back.

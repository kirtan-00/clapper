What the shape says

App opens outnumber landings

88 opens against 76 landings. Most sessions never touch the landing page at all — they come from an installed home-screen icon opening the app directly. The marketing site and the product already have different audiences, and the product's is bigger.

The core loop completes

67 cuts against 69 rolls. Effectively every take that starts also gets closed properly. Whatever is wrong with the rolling screen's layout, it is not stopping people finishing a take.

No outside audience yet

Strip the deploy day and you have roughly four landings a day. clapper.in is 2 days old and the nine guides went up with it. Nothing has been crawled or ranked. This is day one, not a bad result.

22 CTA clicks, 25 projects

Almost everyone who crosses from the site into the app does create a project. The leak is upstream, at getting anyone to the site at all — not in the first-run experience.

Three things these numbers cannot tell you

Views, never people

There is no device or anonymous ID. One person opening the site seven times and seven people opening it once are the same row. "Distinct users" counts signed-in accounts only, which is why it reads 1–4. Adding an anonymous ID is an open decision, and it is a privacy call as much as a technical one.

The nine guides are unmeasured

The landing_view beacon lives in the shared page tail and the five template pages. The articles never got it. That is why the path breakdown puts 272 of 275 views on / and zero on every guide — the best SEO surface is the one the database cannot see. Google Analytics covers them from 24 Aug; the first-party table does not.

Sessions are not sessions

The new session_end event fires when the tab goes to the background. On set a phone locks between every take, so one real sitting produces several rows. It answers "which screen do people fall out on" and must never be totalled as a session count.
